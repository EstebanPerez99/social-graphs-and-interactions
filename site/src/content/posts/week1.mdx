## What we asked

Wikipedia links have a direction. When the Hulk article mentions Spider-Man, that is one link, Hulk → Spider-Man: Spider-Man *receives* it, Hulk *sends* it. So every character in the playground carries two numbers — how many articles point at them (**in-degree**) and how many they point at (**out-degree**).

Our question for week 1: **do those two numbers measure the same thing?** If "being well-connected" were a single property of a character, the two distributions should have the same shape, and the same names should sit at the top of both lists.

## What we did

We loaded the frozen snapshot as a directed graph, taking the node list from the roster so the isolated characters survive. Then we computed both degrees for every character, plotted each distribution on linear and log–log axes, and overlaid the course's binning scheme on the raw counts.

## What surprised us

**1. They are two different shapes.** In-degree has a heavy tail: most characters are linked by a handful of articles, and then there is Spider-Man, linked by 106 — more than a third of the entire category. On log–log axes the in-degree cloud drops in a near-straight line across a full decade. Out-degree looks nothing like that: it rises to a peak around 3, falls off, and *stops* at 28. Nobody links to more than 28 other superheroes.

The reading that convinced us: out-degree is bounded by **effort** — one article, written by a finite number of editors, can only mention so many characters. In-degree is bounded only by the **size of the universe**. Attention is free to concentrate; writing is not.

**2. The famous do not link back.** Of the 106 articles that link to Spider-Man, his own article links back to 9. That is 8% reciprocity for the biggest hub in the network — against 39% for the network as a whole. Hulk returns 14% of his links, Doctor Strange 20%. Fame in a link graph is largely one-way traffic.

**3. The talkers are not the famous.** The characters who send the most links are a different crowd from the ones who receive them:

| top out-degree | out | in |   | top in-degree | in | out |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| Betsy Braddock | 28 | 7 |   | Spider-Man | 106 | 9 |
| Cloak and Dagger | 24 | 11 |   | Hulk | 64 | 10 |
| Adam Warlock | 22 | 8 |   | Wolverine | 60 | 17 |
| Venom | 21 | 17 |   | Doctor Strange | 50 | 17 |
| She-Hulk | 20 | 29 |   | Deadpool | 33 | 19 |

Betsy Braddock's article mentions 28 heroes; 7 mention her. If we had collapsed the graph to undirected, she and Deadpool would look similar. They are not.

**4. Seventeen characters are not in the story at all.** Baymax, Super Rabbit, Miracleman, Yo-Yo Rodriguez — no article in the category links to them, and they link to no one. Being in *Category:Marvel Comics superheroes* is an editorial decision; being part of the network is a different one. This is also why we built the graph from the roster and not from the edge list: the edge list alone would silently drop them and report 286 nodes.

A smaller surprise, about the method rather than the data: the binned line only tells a clean story because every count is divided by its bin width. Skip that step and the doubling bins in the tail jump up by 8×, 16×, 32× — and manufacture a bump that is not there.
